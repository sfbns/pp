## Start with a question brief

Before delegation or drafting, state:

- the user's decision or output;
- the visible object or conventional claim;
- the candidate canonical benchmark;
- the suspected omitted wedge;
- the setting, agents, outcome, and available observables;
- what is known, provisional, and missing;
- whether the task asks for explanation, a testable hypothesis, a formal model, a structural counterfactual, or paper-ready prose.

Do not send a specialist a generic request to “read papers.” Give it one or more questions from the protocol and require a source-linked memo.

## Build the theory-to-hypothesis chain

Require this sequence:

`real problem -> canonical benchmark -> primitive held fixed -> omitted wedge -> corrected object -> primitives and timing -> arrow-by-arrow mechanism -> proposition or directional hypothesis -> observable implication -> rival and falsifier -> estimand -> identification boundary`

The “economics foundation” must constrain the argument. It must identify the benchmark result, the primitive or friction being changed, and why the sign, heterogeneity, nonlinearity, or equilibrium response follows. Do not use theory names as decorative labels after an intuition has already been chosen.

Separate four outputs:

1. a model implication or proposition;
2. a testable empirical hypothesis;
3. a measurement mapping;
4. a causal or structural interpretation.

Never turn one into another without the required assumptions.

## Package the hypothesis

Use a compact top-journal logic when supported:

1. establish the important phenomenon or policy object;
2. show why the standard benchmark is attractive but incomplete;
3. introduce one consequential wedge, not a list of vaguely related frictions;
4. derive the mechanism link by link from primitives and timing;
5. state the main prediction plus the heterogeneity, boundary, or equilibrium prediction that distinguishes the mechanism;
6. connect each prediction to an observable and a design;
7. state the nearest rival and the pattern that would weaken the preferred story;
8. claim only the contribution supported by the evidence.

Prefer a corrected economic object over topic-only novelty. Do not reverse-engineer a theory label from a convenient regression sign.

## Evidence and writeback boundaries

- Label source-verified claims, card-backed synthesis, and new inference separately.
- Treat OCR, batch synthesis, and candidate cards as retrieval aids.
- Do not call a paper newly read in this run unless the original full text was actually opened and checked with anchors.
- Do not invent propositions, page numbers, equation numbers, findings, or citations.
- Specialist memories are curated read-only inputs during normal use. Additions require a new reviewed bridge card, its source lineage, and an explicit promotion decision; never silently overwrite them.
- End with the surviving mechanism, weakest link, discriminating test, and the exact additional evidence needed.
