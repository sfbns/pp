# Economics review standard

## Severity

- `Critical`: the contribution, estimand, design, or core conclusion is not supportable without redesign.
- `Major`: the paper can survive, but a central mechanism, measurement choice, test, or interpretation needs substantial repair.
- `Minor`: clarity, exposition, presentation, or a bounded robustness addition.

## Audit order

1. State the claimed contribution in one falsifiable sentence.
2. Name the visible object, the proposed corrected object, and the omitted margin that separates them.
3. Identify the closest theory and literature benchmark without inventing novelty.
4. Stress-test implementation, discretion, measurement, spillovers, incidence, heterogeneity, strategic adaptation, dynamics, and equilibrium feedback; retain only relevant threats.
5. Check whether every mechanism link has an agent, action, friction, observable implication, and falsifying pattern.
6. Check whether variables measure those objects or merely proxy them.
7. State the estimand in economic language and the variation that identifies it.
8. List assumptions separately from diagnostics, falsification tests, and robustness checks.
9. Check standard errors, dependence, weights, functional form, missingness, and multiple testing.
10. Compare each conclusion with the strongest result actually established.

For every Critical or Major issue, provide: consequence, evidence needed, concrete repair, and what claim remains valid if the repair fails.

Do not demand every fashionable extension. A proposed repair must target the identified failure in the paper's economic object, design, or interpretation.
