## Owned decisions

1. [inference] Choose the order in which the reader encounters visible problem, benchmark, fixed primitive, wedge, mechanism, prediction, rival, contribution, and boundary.
2. [inference] Assign one rhetorical job to every sentence and remove sentences that do not advance the chain.
3. [inference] Keep the same nouns for the research object, primitive, action, and outcome so the mechanism does not drift through synonyms.
4. [inference] Make the condition for sign, threshold, or policy ranking visible in the hypothesis sentence rather than a footnote.
5. [inference] Place citations where they establish benchmark results, institutional facts, and neighboring corrections; avoid author-by-author catalogues.
6. [inference] Match verbs to evidence status: predicts/characterizes for theory; estimates/identifies only for supported designs; is consistent with for mechanism evidence; model implies for counterfactual or welfare objects.
7. [inference] Frame contribution as a corrected object or primitive relative to the nearest literature, not “we combine X and Y.”
8. [inference] Preserve the nearest rival and boundary in the main argument even when word limits are tight.

## Seven-sentence hypothesis packaging template

1. [inference] **Visible benchmark**: “In the canonical [theory] framework, [shock/policy] should [baseline result] because [decision condition].”
2. [inference] **Fixed primitive**: “That result holds [information/contract space/outside option/composition/strategy/price/equilibrium response] fixed.”
3. [inference] **Institutional wedge**: “In this setting, [verified institutional feature] makes that primitive endogenous through [minimal wedge].”
4. [inference] **Actor-level bridge**: “The wedge changes [agent]’s [information/constraint/incentive], inducing [action], which changes [market/institution response].”
5. [inference] **Conditional hypothesis**: “Accordingly, [observable contrast] should be [signed] when [condition], but attenuate or reverse when [boundary].”
6. [inference] **Rival discriminator**: “Unlike [closest rival], this mechanism also predicts [timing/distribution/footprint/heterogeneity].”
7. [inference] **Permission and contribution**: “The design identifies [estimand]; [mediator/structural/welfare layer] is [mechanism-consistent/model-implied], and the contribution is to correct [old object/primitive], not to establish [forbidden upgrade].”

### Sentence ledger

| sentence | must contain | must not contain | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | recognized result and why | long author list | inference |
| 2 | one fixed primitive | generic “prior work overlooks complexity” | inference |
| 3 | source-backed institutional fact | invented policy detail | inference |
| 4 | actors, choices, arrows | passive “thereby affects” chain | inference |
| 5 | sign plus condition plus observable | vague moderator | inference |
| 6 | nearest rival plus unique contrast | weakest imaginable alternative | inference |
| 7 | estimand, evidence label, corrected contribution | causal/welfare overclaim | inference |

## Reusable writing actions

1. [inference] **Object-name freeze**: choose one phrase each for benchmark object, primitive, wedge, action, and outcome; reuse them exactly.
2. [inference] **Arrow-verb audit**: underline every changes, induces, raises, lowers, reallocates, or selects; require an actor, object, and evidence/theory basis.
3. [inference] **Sign-condition visibility check**: the reader must find the condition without consulting a footnote, appendix, or later heterogeneity section.
4. [inference] **Review-to-contribution chain**: organize citations by propositions—benchmark, correction, measurement, rival—then state the paper’s marginal change to the object.
5. [inference] **Evidence-verb substitution**: replace demonstrates with predicts, estimates, is consistent with, or model implies according to the referee’s ledger.
6. [inference] **Module-job assignment**: say which dataset/model component establishes benchmark fact, measures primitive, tests action, distinguishes rival, or carries welfare.
7. [inference] **Benchmark-preserving critique**: state what the old design still estimates before stating what it cannot identify; this sharpens rather than caricatures the gap.
8. [inference] **Boundary compression**: if space is scarce, retain sign condition, closest rival, estimand, and forbidden upgrade before secondary citations.
9. [inference] **Paragraph back-translation**: remove all economics labels temporarily; if constraints, information, choices, and equilibrium disappear, the passage is decorative.

## Failure modes and evidence boundaries

- [inference] Do not begin with a catalogue of authors when the reader needs a benchmark and tension.
- [inference] Do not vary names for the same construct until the mechanism appears to contain several objects.
- [inference] Do not hide the sign condition in a robustness paragraph.
- [inference] Do not transform mechanism-consistent evidence into mediation or a model-implied welfare object into an observed outcome.
- [inference] Do not call simulation, calibration, or fit identification.
- [inference] Do not write “theory suggests” when no agent, constraint, information set, or equilibrium object changes.
- [inference] Do not portray the benchmark as wrong if the bridge card says it remains valid for a narrower estimand or limiting case.
- [bridge-card synthesis] Do not quote or reproduce exact mathematical conditions from these cards without reopening the original source.
- [inference] Do not let polished prose rescue an incomplete handoff. Missing primitive, condition, falsifier, source anchor, or evidence status is a stop condition.
