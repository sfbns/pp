# Evidence boundaries

## Literature completion

Do not label a paper fully read or memorized unless it has all of the following:

1. complete full text rather than abstract-only or OCR-only screening;
2. an individual paper package;
3. chunk or page anchors for material claims;
4. a completed per-paper card;
5. an explicit completion state.

Batch summaries, candidate lists, OCR output, and first-pass cards remain intermediate evidence.

The local top-journal collection contains 100 AER third-pass paper memories and 107 AEJ second-pass cards. These are strong routing and comparison layers, but they are not 207 independently source-verified papers:

- The AER third-pass queue is complete at the memory-production layer. The 100 recorded AER source-PDF paths and the 107 AEJ source-PDF paths were present when this skill was updated, but verify the relevant path live before relying on it. Reopen the paper memory and source pack, then inspect the PDF for consequential formulas, tables, figures, proofs, quotations, and layout claims.
- The AEJ library is explicitly full-OCR second-pass memory. Treat its cards and synthesis as card-backed or memory-routed unless the individual source and anchors have been checked for the present claim.
- Never translate `status: done` for a memory artifact into `source-verified` for every claim in the underlying paper.

## Formula completion

Use OCR to locate formulas. Verify consequential equations against the page image or a clean source. Check notation, subscripts, conditioning sets, support, normalization, and limiting arguments. Label unresolved formulas provisional.

## Claim ladder

- `source-verified`: supported by an inspected original source or anchored full-text package.
- `card-backed`: supported by a reviewed local card whose scope and boundary are known.
- `memory-routed`: useful for locating evidence but not sufficient for a precise claim.
- `inference`: the expert's synthesis; label it as interpretation rather than source fact.

Cross-paper regularities such as object correction, implementation gaps, or strategic adaptation are `inference` unless the answer reopens and cites the supporting paper-level evidence.
