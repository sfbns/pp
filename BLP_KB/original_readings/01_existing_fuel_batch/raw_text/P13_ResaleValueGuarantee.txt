Abstract
A resale value guaranteed (RVG) strategy has emerged as a novel way to alleviate EV pur-
chasers’ resale anxiety. However, certain questions, such as when to initiate a RVG strategy 
and whether to share information with supply chain partners, are unanswered. In this study, 
we aim to investigate the impacts of a RVG strategy and reliability of a RVG decision on 
EV adoption and supply chain performance. We develop analytical models to analyze the 
optimal RVG strategy in asymmetric versus symmetric information situations, using the 
traditional business model as a benchmark. The results indicate that a RVG strategy can be 
an effective way to increase supply chain profits for products that are subject to a degree of 
resale anxiety, trade-in cost, and the reliability of the manufacturer’s commitments. Incen-
tives provided by one party to the other can be utilized to encourage information sharing. 
However, information sharing does not necessarily lead to higher sales volume.
Keywords  Electric vehicle · Resale anxiety · Resale value guaranteed · Information 
sharing · Decision reliability

information situation?

on supply chain performance when information is shared between supply chain partners, 
and nothing on how it affects EV adoption in practice.

2  Research background and literature review
Implementing a resale value guaranteed strategy has emerged as a new way to alleviate 
consumers’ resale anxiety. Our research of RVG strategy mostly relates to three areas: 
money-back guarantee, information sharing, and resale anxiety.
In the money-back guarantee literature, Hsiao and Chen (2012) investigate the rationale 
behind the different return policies of Internet sellers. Their analysis shows that a MBG 
policy is necessary only when consumers have a higher “hassle cost”, and when the seller 
is thought to take high-quality risk may offer higher-than-price refund. Akçay et al. (2013) 
propose a model to identify the impacts of MBG policies on sales quantity, price, and 
firms’ performance. They show that a MBG policy can increase sales and profit for the 
dealer, while the resale of returned products can decrease stocking levels. Chen and Chen 
(2017) study MBG policy as a channel selection problem. They propose that the dealer 
should offer a MBG as long as the salvage value of the returned goods in a channel is posi-
tive. MBGs in these studies are initiated by the dealer, because the dealer possesses more 
market information, and most of MBG-covered products are fast-moving consumer goods. 
However, in the EV industry, a RVG strategy is normally initiated by the EV makers, as 
they are more motivated to sell EVs and have more capacity to reallocate the returned vehi-
cles to other businesses, such as leasing.
Few researchers have studied the impacts of MBG on manufacturers’ operational per-
formance. There are notable exceptions. Crocker and Letizia (2014) propose that the opti-
mal return policy for a manufacturer is to refund the full wholesale price for any returns, 
together with a bonus paid to the dealer. Li et al. (2018) propose a dual-channel supply 
chain, with the manufacturer selling both high- and low-quality products. Letizia et  al. 
(2018) further show that flexibility in reducing returns through a dual channel structure 
can be the most profitable way for the manufacturer. However, information sharing is not 
a focus of their research. In fact, few current MBG studies consider information sharing 
as an influential factor. Information sharing is a concern, because manufacturers who are 
considering a RVG or MBG policy are not close to the market, and have to contend with 
absent or unreliable consumer information.
The second category of related literature is in the field of information sharing. The 
research into information sharing in business has a long history. In general, it has been 
shown that firms can benefit from information sharing if prices and ordering information

designed sharing mechanism (Clarke 1983). Hence many efforts have been made to con-

chain. A stream of researchers has shown that some signals concerning advance quantity 
commitments (Li et al. 2014; Özer and Wei 2006), production capacity (Liu et al. 2020a, 
b), cheap talk (Chu et al. 2017), or supply chain contracts (Ha and Tong 2008), are helpful 
in designing effective information sharing mechanisms. Other researchers demonstrate that 
incentives such as side payments can be effective in encouraging the dealer to share market 
information (Jiang and Hao 2016; Zhang 2002). Sometime the dealer may voluntarily share 
his or her private market information with the manufacturer when the dealer has imperfect 
demand information and there is a significant level of market demand uncertainty (Li and 
Zhang 2015).
All these studies share a common assumption that decisions made on the information 
that will be shared is reliable. However, this might not always be the case, and inaccu-
rate information could lead to unreliable decision. Chen et al. (2001) study two competing 
firms, and show that imperfect targeting of consumers’ information and unreliable demand 
information can adversely affect firms’ competitive strategies. We take a different angle 
by studying a supply chain that consists of one EV maker and one dealer, to investigate 
whether information sharing is beneficial to the implementation of a RVG strategy. In addi-
tion, the EV industry is different from the subjects of the research cite above with addi-
tional consideration of resale anxiety and reliability of decisions, which have significant 
impacts on the demand function and operational performance.
The third related area considers EV resale anxiety. In psychology, anxiety is consid-
ered to be one of the sources of consumer prejudice, and the existence of such prejudice 
often leads to negative results (Svenson 1981; Taylor and Brown 1988). In the EV industry, 
resale anxiety arises from consumers’ concerns about the rapid depreciation of an EV’s 
value. Although the negative impacts of resale anxiety have been widely reported, it is only 
recently that resale anxiety has been captured in OM research. Lim et al. (2015) take resale 
anxiety to be the main influencing on people choosing between different EV battery mod-
els. In this paper, we consider the influence of resale anxiety on the reliability of decision 
making in the EV supply chain implementing a RVG strategy.
3  The model

a constant marginal cost c. A risk neutral dealer orders EVs from the manufacturer using a 
wholesale contract. The supply chain partners seek to maximize their own profits in asym-
metry versus symmetry information situations.

6  Concluding remarks
In this study, we investigate the impacts of a resale value guaranteed strategy and infor-
mation sharing on EV adoption and supply chain performance. To do so, we develop 
noncooperative game models to analyze the optimal RVG strategy in asymmetric versus 
symmetric information situations, using the traditional business model as a benchmark. 
The results of our analysis demonstrate that a RVG strategy can be an effective way to 
increase supply chain profits, subject to the degree of resale anxiety, trade-in cost, and 
reliability of the manufacturer’s decision as to whether to implement such a strategy. 
Incentives provided by the manufacturer to the dealer can be utilized to encourage infor-
mation sharing. However, information sharing does not necessarily lead to higher sales 
volume. We show that information sharing reduces the manufacturer’s uncertainty in 
estimating the dealer’s response, and hence leads to a reduced expected sales quantity. 
Our results highlight the interdependence of resale anxiety and reliability of the manu-
facturer’s decision made in the RVG supply chain.
Our study has important implications for those in the supply chain. Firms that are 
running a sales business in the EV market should acknowledge that EVs’ resale value 
has significant impacts on the demand function. Hence firms should consider whether 
to adopt a RVG strategy, as such a strategy can be an effective way to reduce consum-
ers’ resale concerns and to increase firm’s profits, as demonstrated in our study. When 
implementing a RVG strategy, attention should be paid to certain key factors, including 
the degree of resale anxiety, the reliability of the RVG strategy decision, and the trade-
in costs. These key factors determine when is the best time to initiate a RVG strategy, 
and whether it is necessary for the manufactuer to provide incentive to the dealer.
As pointed out in our analyses, EV makers have to reallocate the returned vehicles to 
other businesses, or resell them onto the market. Whether to adopt a RVG strategy when 
selling both new and used EVs could be an interesting future research topic.
