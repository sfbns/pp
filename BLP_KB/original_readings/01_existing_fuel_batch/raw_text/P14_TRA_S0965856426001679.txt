A B S T R A C T
Vehicle-to-Grid (V2G) technology enables bidirectional energy flow between electric vehicles 
(EVs) and the grid, leveraging parked vehicles for energy management and grid stability. As a 
leading nation in EV and renewable electricity adoption, China is well-positioned to develop and 
implement V2G technology. However, the success of V2G technology hinges on public engage­
ment. This study examines the public’s willingness to engage with V2G technology in China. 
Through a web-based stated choice survey, we explore consumer preferences regarding V2G at­
tributes and latent variables using a hybrid choice model (HCM). The results highlight the 
importance of plugged-in hours and economic incentives in shaping public willingness to engage 
with V2G. Regarding latent variables, “Benefits”, “Concerns”, and “Technology affinity” are 
significantly related. The Willingness to Accept (WTA) was also calculated, revealing the mone­
tary compensation consumers expect to be paid with the changes of V2G contract attributes 
levels. Additionally, pseudo-elasticity analysis quantifies how changes in specific attributes affect 
the probability of consumer engagement, providing further insights for policy and incentive 
design. In conclusion, this research provides actionable insights for policymakers to enhance V2G 
adoption and customer satisfaction.
1. Introduction
The global pursuit of carbon neutrality has catalyzed an unprecedented acceleration in energy transition, marked by rapid de­
velopments in renewable energy sources and electric vehicle (EV) adoption. However, this transformation is not without its challenges. 
The inherent variability and unpredictability of renewable energy sources, coupled with the uncertain electricity charging demand of 
EVs, have introduced new complexities to the management and stability of power systems (Wei et al., 2022).
Vehicle-to-Grid systems have emerged as a promising solution to these challenges, offering dynamic interactions between EVs and 
the power grid (Qin et al., 2023). In a broad sense, Vehicle-to-Grid includes V1G and V2G: V1G is a technology that controls the 
charging rate and time of electricity directed from the grid to the EV battery; V2G facilitates bidirectional power flow, allowing EVs to 
both draw power from and supply power back to the grid (Barman et al., 2023). For the purposes of this paper, we will focus 
exclusively on V2G. As an interactive mechanism between EVs and the grid, V2G systems can play a significant role in peak shaving,

most days, implementing V2G during these periods of inactivity could significantly alleviate issues related to energy management and 
grid stability. Currently, there are over 140 Vehicle-to-Grid projects worldwide, with most being proof of concept trials or small-scale 
commercial trials (V2Ghub, 2024). V2G has been widely demonstrated to be technically feasible. However, one of the significant 
barriers to the large-scale commercialization of V2G is the relatively low level of public engagement. Understanding public partici­

development of more effective policies and incentives, thereby enhancing the adoption of V2G technology and strengthening national

Although previous studies have explored the effects of various factors on people’s acceptance of V2G, comprehensive insights into 
these factors, particularly within the context of the Chinese market, remain limited. Furthermore, many papers have mentioned that 
battery degradation is a significant barrier to people's participation in V2G, yet no one has quantitatively described the public's 
tolerance level for battery degradation. China, being the largest new energy vehicle market globally, has created a favorable envi­
ronment for the implementation of V2G technology. Additionally, as the largest supplier of renewable electricity (IRENA, 2024), China 
has cultivated an urgent need for energy storage technologies such as V2G for coping with renewable energy fluctuation. This paper 
pioneers in empirically explore the public's willingness to engage with V2G in China, shedding new lights on the various influencing 
attributes. A web-based stated choice survey is designed to understand the trade-offs between different factors influencing consumer 
acceptance and to identify opportunities for enhancing the adoption of V2G systems. The innovative use of battery degradation 
warranties in our survey design allows us to quantitatively assess consumers' tolerance for battery degradation, a critical concern that 
has been qualitatively discussed but seldom measured. Employing a hybrid choice model (HCM), we conduct a detailed analysis of 
respondents' preferences and decision-making processes concerning V2G technology. Unlike conventional discrete choice models that 
assume fully rational decisions based solely on observable attributes, HCM integrates both observable choices and latent psychological 
constructs This enhanced explanatory power enables a more comprehensive understanding of the behavioral mechanisms underlying 
consumers’ acceptance of V2G, which allows policymakers to design targeted interventions (e.g., awareness campaigns, information 
transparency) accordingly. Subsequently, we calculate the attribute importance factor and the willingness to acceptance (WTA) of 
V2G. By understanding the specific compensations required for different attributes, program designers can better communicate the 
benefits and tailor their offerings to meet user expectations, thus enhancing overall engagement and satisfaction. In addition, we 
employ pseudo-elasticity analysis to quantify how changes in specific V2G attributes affect the probability of consumer participation, 
providing actionable insights for policy design and incentive optimization.
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: The next section presents a literature review on the topic, followed by an 
exposition of our survey design and data collection methodology. Subsequent sections detail the model approach, and then present the 
model estimation results. The paper concludes with a synthesis of findings and considerations for future policy development.
2. Literature review
Scholars have examined various factors that influence individual and organizational decisions to engage with V2G systems. This 
literature review traces the evolution of V2G acceptance from its inception to the present day. The pioneering work of Parson et al. 
(2014) utilized two separated but logically connected stated preference (SP) experiments to evaluate the impact of V2G capabilities on 
the marketability of EVs and people’s preference for V2G contract attributes in the United States, with results indicating that V2G did 
not significantly influence EV preferences. Hidrue & Parsons (2015) used contingent valuation method to study consumer willingness 
to pay for V2G-enabled EVs. The results showed across all scenarios considered, the prices consumers were willing to pay for V2G- 
enabled EVs were lower than the projected costs due to range anxiety, stringent V2G contracts, and high battery costs. Kubli et al. 
(2018) found that individuals exhibit a greater propensity to engage in distributed energy presumptions (V2G) compared to heat pump 
systems in Switzerland. Geske & Schumann (2018) revealed that 'range anxiety' and guaranteed ‘minimum range’ are dominant issues 
for potential EV adopters to accept V2G in Germany, with limited support from remuneration. In addition to EV users, experts are also 
valuable interview subjects. Kester et al. (2018) and Sovacool et al. (2019) interviewed 227 experts in transportation and electricity 
across Nordic countries, revealing debates surrounding V2G and divergent perspectives across different countries.
In recent 5 years, there has been a growing body of literature focusing on V2G participation. Lee et al. (2020) utilized the contingent 
valuation approach to assess the willingness to accept V2G technologies among Korean households, finding a mean estimated will­
ingness to accept was to be KRW 9821 (USD 8.83) per month per vehicle. Van Heuveln et al. (2021) interviewed 20 Dutch EV drivers 
and found that factors like financial compensation, transparent communication, and reliable control enhance acceptance of V2G 
technology, while range anxiety, discomfort, and battery degradation concerns hinder it. Huang et al. (2021) found a significant 
reluctance among Dutch EV drivers to participate in V2G programs. However, the study also noted that the general preference for V2G 
could increase substantially if fast charging is widely available in the future. Baumgartner (2022) utilized a moderation model to reveal 
that familiarity with EVs positively influences V2G acceptance. Different from previous studies that lacked hands-on V2G experience, 
Ghotge et al. (2022) invited seventeen Dutch participants to experience V2G technology firsthand, revealing that transparent 
communication on battery impacts, adequate financial compensation, real-time battery monitoring, and user-friendly controls are keys 
to enhancing user acceptance of V2G. Bohdanowicz et al. (2022) made a study across Spain, France, Italy, and Denmark and found that 
individuals with extrinsic financial motivations were sensitive to incentives, while those with intrinsic motivations, such as controlling 
spending or ecological concerns, showed interest regardless of reward size. Conversely, individuals with general modesty motivations 
might be discouraged by financial incentives. Mehdizadeh et al. (2023) used a multi-equation econometric model to estimate mini­
mum guaranteed charges and financial compensations for V2G technology in Norway. The study's findings indicated that that on

Although this body of literature has grown increasingly sophisticated, several critical gaps remain. First, most studies rely on 
simplified economic or technical attributes without systematically incorporating latent psychological variables such as technology 
trust, perceived benefit, or environmental identity. This limits understanding of how attitudinal factors mediate observed choices. 
Second, while numerous studies acknowledge battery degradation as a major barrier to V2G participation, very few quantify how 
consumers value or tolerate such degradation. Most existing work treats degradation as a qualitative concern rather than a measurable 
decision variable (Hu et al., 2025). Consequently, the monetary valuation of battery degradation—that is, how much compensation 
consumers require to offset perceived or real losses in battery life—remains a largely unaddressed issue in the V2G acceptance 
literature. Concerns about battery degradation are not unique to V2G but are deeply rooted in broader EV acceptance research. While 
some studies have examined the effects of battery warranties and depreciation rates on EV purchase intentions (Li et al., 2020), few 
have translated these technical metrics into explicit financial valuations. Third, much of the research focuses on developed economies 
such as the United States, Germany, and the Netherlands. In contrast, developing or rapidly transitioning markets like China, which 
represent the world’s largest base of EV users and renewable electricity producers, have been underrepresented. The behavioral 
patterns, cultural norms, and policy contexts in such markets may differ substantially, limiting the transferability of Western findings.
In summary, while past studies have advanced knowledge about V2G acceptance, they often treat battery degradation as an ab­
stract deterrent and overlook the attitudinal dynamics underlying participation. This paper contributes to filling these voids by 
explicitly valuing consumers’ tolerance for degradation, incorporating latent psychological factors into behavioral modeling, and 
situating the analysis within the Chinese context—where policy frameworks, market scale, and energy priorities collectively create a 
unique environment for V2G development.
3. Survey design and data collection
The survey of this study consists of three parts, basic information of the respondents (such as age, gender, education level, income, 
etc.), latent variables, and stated choice experiment. Basic information helps analyze differences in behavioral patterns among 
different demographic groups. Latent variables allow for a deeper exploration of respondents' psychological and cognitive dimensions. 
Stated choice experiments provides insights into consumer preferences and decision-making behavior for products that have not yet 
been widely adopted in real life. By presenting respondents with hypothetical scenarios and asking them to make choices among 
alternatives with different attribute combinations, stated choice experiments simulate real-world decision-making processes.
3.1. Latent variables
Latent variables represent factors that cannot be directly measured. Researching the impact of latent variables on V2G participation 
helps to better understand the underlying factors that influence public involvement in V2G, and thus to design targeted promotional 
strategies. The indicators of the latent variables are shown in Table 1.
V2G offers many benefits, and how respondents value these benefits influences their willingness to engage in V2G. While Geske & 
Schumann (2018) collected data on the benefits of V2G, they did not treat them as latent variables but rather included them in a 
regression model by coding the 7-point Likert scale as seven binary variables. In this study, we model “Benefits” as a latent variable and 
use a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from “not important at all” to “very important,” to assess how respondents value these benefits.

3.2. SP experiment design
Before the SP experiment, we used visual aids to introduce general information about V2G, helping respondents gain a clearer 
understanding of what V2G is. The visual aid, depicted in Fig. 1, provided a clear overview of how V2G functions. In this system, EV 
owners connect their vehicles to the grid for a specified period. During this time, a third party, such as an energy provider, manages the 
charging and discharging of the vehicle's battery. This process allows for the integration of stored energy into the grid during peak 
demand periods and draws energy from the grid when demand is low. In exchange for participating in this system, vehicle owners 
receive financial incentives.
To help respondents understand the SP experiment, an example of an SP choice experiment was provided. The screenshot of the 
example is shown in Fig. 2. Respondents were presented with three options: the first two were hypothetical V2G contracts, and the 
third option was an opt-out alternative. Respondents were asked which option they would choose, assuming they have an electric 
vehicle with a range of 500 km. The meaning of each attribute was explained to the respondents. This preparatory step was designed to 
familiarize respondents with the experiment’s structure and the relevant attributes, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of 
their responses.
Revenue is one of the most frequently considered factors in SP experiments about V2G. Understanding the dynamics of revenue is 
crucial for the development of effective V2G business models. The actual revenue a V2G-EV can earn depends on many factors, 
including the type of power market, the region of the country (different regions have different A/S prices), power capacity of the 
connection, and others (Mak et al., 2024). Parson et al. (2014) explored people's reactions to V2G earnings, presenting scenarios with 
annual earnings of $500 to $5000. Geske & Schumann (2018) investigated the impact of revenue on people's choices by considering 
monthly incomes of €0-€60 and a one-time premium of €1000- €7000 for buying a car. Huang et al. (2021) examined the effects of fixed 
monthly revenue of €20-€100 plus a variable cost of €0-€0.3 per extra hour outside of the contract. Bohdanowicz et al. (2022) took a 
different approach by measuring the impact of additional (2.5/5/10/15/20)% free electricity as a reward on people's participation in 
V2G. In this paper, we investigate the spectrum of consumer reactions to annual revenue, which is set between ¥1000 and ¥5000, to 
provide insights into the economic dimensions influencing V2G adoption.

The average daily plugged-in time refers to the average time an EV remains connected to a V2G charger on a daily basis. If an EV is 
connected for fewer hours on certain days, the shortfall can be compensated on other days. The range of 5 to 20 h was explored by 
Parson et al. (2014) to assess respondents' acceptance levels of daily plugged-in hours. Geske & Schumann (2018) and Huang et al. 
(2021) conducted similar explorations with the ranges being 5–––14 h and 5––15 h, respectively. To explore the preferences and 
adaptability of Chinese respondents, we introduced a variable charging duration ranging from 4 to 16 h.
Regarding whether V2G leads to battery degradation, some studies suggest that V2G could contribute to battery degradation 
(Dubarry et al., 2018; Thingvad et al., 2021), while some others concluded that V2G has the potential to improve battery lifetime under 
smart management (Uddin et al., 2018; Gong et al., 2024). Factors affecting battery degradation are varied and include temperature, 
charging rate, depth of discharge, and usage patterns, among others. Estimating the extent to which V2G might shorten battery life is 
challenging. As Liao et al. (2017) said, there are a lot of uncertainties regarding battery life and consumers may prefer more certainty 
for these aspects. Several studies have explored user preferences regarding discharging cycles in V2G context to understand attitudes 
towards battery degradation (Kubli et al., 2018; Geske & Schumann, 2018). However, the term 'cycle life limits' is quite technical and 
not widely known among general customers, and it changes significantly with the rapid advancement of technology. To measure 
people's acceptance of battery degradation, this study employs the concept of a battery degradation warranty to transform this un­
certain variable into a certain one. The battery degradation warranty period is defined as the duration of time during which the 
manufacturer is obligated to repair or replace the battery if it degrades. In this study, we explore the responses to different levels of 
battery degradation warranty period, categorized into four levels spanning 6 to 9 years. Battery degradation warranty is different from 
general battery warranty. General battery warranty period has been found to have a positive effect on the purchase of EVs (Mau et al., 
2008). Since 2016, China has mandated that automobile manufacturers must provide a minimum of 8-year (or 120,000-km) battery 
warranty (The State Council of China, 2024), which does not cover battery degradation. This study will explore whether and how the 
battery degradation warranty period affects the willingness to participate in V2G.

3.3. Data collection
China leads the world in EV sales, with Guangdong Province being the top region in the country for EV sales. By 2023, the number 
of EVs in Guangdong Province reached 2.89 million (Guangdong Provincial Government, 2024). This advanced state of vehicle 
electrification has laid the groundwork for the implementation of V2G technology. Focusing on the Guangdong area, this study ex­
amines the impact of various contract conditions on the willingness to participate in V2G among current EV drivers and those 
intending to purchase EVs in the future.
Before conducting the formal data collection, we performed a pilot test from April 14th to April 30th, 2024. This pilot phase 
allowed us to gather feedback from respondents, which we used to refine and improve our questionnaire. The formal data collection 
was subsequently conducted in Guangdong, China, between May 1st to June 30th, 2024, using the web-based survey system Credamo. 
The platform initially targeted participants to match the demographic distribution of Guangdong Province. Two screening questions 
were then applied: respondents were asked whether they currently own an EV and whether they intend to purchase an EV in the future. 
Only participants who answered “yes” to at least one of these questions were qualified to proceed as respondents. To ensure data 
quality, we incorporated a simple arithmetic question. Respondents who answered this question incorrectly were automatically dis­
qualified and their responses were deemed invalid. Additionally, responses with excessively short completion time and those with 
overly patterned answers were also considered invalid. Ultimately, 2,668 observations were obtained from 667 effective respondents. 
To verify the representativeness of our sample, we compared respondents’ key characteristics with those reported in China NEV 
consumer profile (IPS Consulting Group, 2024). The comparison showed that the distributions of major attributes were broadly 
similar, suggesting that our sample is reasonably representative.
Table 3 provides a comprehensive overview of survey respondents across various demographics and other characteristics. 
Regarding age distribution, the majority of respondents, 67.9%, are under 40 years old, with 32.1% aged 40 and above. This indicates 
that current EV drivers and individuals interested in purchasing EVs in the future are predominantly younger, with a significant 
concentration under 40 years old. Male respondents make up 59.6% of the sample, while females account for 40.3%. In terms of 
education, 60.3% have a university-level education or above, while 39.7% have education below university level. This indicates that 
current EV drivers and individuals interested in purchasing EVs in the future are largely composed of highly educated individuals. 
Concerning annual income, 56.7% of respondents earn less than 150,000 RMB annually, while 43.3% earn 150,000 RMB or more. 
Finally, in terms of knowledge about V2G, 22.5% had never heard of it before, 61.5% know a little about it, and 16.0% are very familiar 
with it.

4.2. HCM
To uncover how the different variables influence the acceptance of V2G, we employ a HCM in this paper. As shown in Fig. 3, the 
HCM is an advanced analytical framework that integrates elements of discrete choice modeling with additional considerations for 
latent variables through a Multiple Indicator-Multiple Cause (MIMIC) model. The discrete choice model focuses on observable at­
tributes, analyzing how different features of a product or service influence preferences. However, it may not fully capture the complex, 
underlying factors that drive these preferences. The MIMIC model addresses this gap by incorporating latent variables, which are 
indirectly measured through multiple indicators. HCM offers deeper insights into both the observable attributes and the latent con­
structs that shape individuals' decisions regarding V2G participation.

5.3. Results of attributes’ importance
The results of attributes’ relative importance factors are shown in Fig. 4. Plugged-in time is shown as the most importance factor, 
indicating that EV drivers are highly concerned about the duration of time required for charging, which may impact their daily routines 
and travel plans. Annual revenue follows as the second most importance attribute. Understanding the importance that users place on 
annual revenue can assist relevant enterprises in designing V2G schemes and pricing strategies that better meet the needs of users, 
thereby enhancing their acceptance and satisfaction with V2G technology. Although not the top priority, the guaranteed minimal 
range is also significant. One potential reason for the divergence from the result of Geske & Schumann (2018), which emphasized the 
guaranteed minimum range as a primary concern, may be that the respondents in our study are informed that participation in V2G 
programs would not compromise their ability to use conventional charging methods. They were informed that as long as the stipulated 
V2G contract duration is met, they retain the flexibility to use standard charging methods at other times. This provision is crucial for 
alleviating concerns about potential limitations on long-distance travel, thereby enhancing the perceived attractiveness of V2G 
participation. Additionally, the battery degradation warranty period and battery capacity under warranty are also important factors. 
Developing appropriate insurance policies that specifically cover battery health in V2G scenarios can minimize user concerns about 
battery degradation and build trust in the technology and its long-term viability.

5.5. Results of pseudo-elasticity
Table 8 presents the pseudo-elasticity effects, which measure the percentage change in the probability when an attribute moves 
from its reference level to the indicated level, holding all else constant. Sociodemographic effects operate indirectly through latent 
variables in the hybrid choice model. Among them, age exhibits a noticeable impact: being under 40 increases the probability of 
choosing V2G by 10.28%. By comparison, gender, education, and income have negligible effects, with elasticity values close to zero. 
The most influential demographic determinant is prior knowledge of V2G. Respondents who had never heard of V2G show a sub­
stantial 24.73% decrease in participation probability, while those who knew only a little about it exhibit an 11.95% reduction, relative 
to individuals who are very familiar with the technology. This underscores the central importance of awareness-building. Similar 
interpretations apply to the contractual attributes. Relative to the ¥5500 reference, shifting the revenue guarantee to ¥1000 lowers the 
participation probability by 9.10%; the penalty shrinks to 5.72% and 2.60% at ¥2500 and ¥4000, respectively. The minimum driving 
range matters equally: a 100-km guarantee reduces participation by 5.70%, whereas 150 km and 200 km cut the penalty to 2.61% and 
1.40% versus the 250-km baseline. Among all contract terms, required plug-in time carries the largest positive elasticity. Mandating 4 
h of daily charging raises participation by 15.73% relative to a 16 h requirement; the gain falls to 11.25% at 8 h and 4.46% at 12 h. 
Battery-related guarantees show notable influences. A 6-year warranty depresses participation by 4.83%; extending coverage to 7 or 8 
years trims the penalty to 3.31% and 3.05% below the 9-year benchmark. Tighter residual-capacity thresholds follow the same 
gradient: guaranteeing 70% capacity lowers participation by 4.55%, whereas 75% and 80% thresholds reduce it by 2.55% and 2.64% 
relative to the 85% reference.
6. Conclusion and policy implications
The deployment of V2G technology has the potential for transforming energy management and consumption. However, without 
realistic estimates regarding the acceptability of V2G, there may be misjudgments about the value and business prospect of V2G. This 
study illuminates the public's willingness to engage with V2G technology within the context of China's burgeoning EV market. Our 
analysis, based in a HCM model, reveals how different factors influence people’s acceptance of V2G. Results show that the key de­
terminants in V2G contract include plug-in time and economic incentives, complemented by the guaranteed minimum driving range, 
battery degradation warranty, and battery capacity under warranty. Regarding latent variables, “Benefits”, “Concerns”, and “Tech­
nology affinity” are significantly related. Younger individuals and those familiar with V2G are more likely to have higher technology 
affinity and perceive its benefits, while individuals with lower educational backgrounds tend to have lower technology affinity and are 
more likely to express concerns about V2G. We also calculated WTA and pseudo-elasticity, which together provide a comprehensive 
understanding for assessing consumer preferences, formulating economic policies, and understanding market dynamics. While WTA 
quantifies the monetary compensation required for changes in contract attributes, the pseudo-elasticity results reveal the sensitivity of 
participation probabilities to these changes. By combining these two measures, stakeholders can design more effective incentive 
mechanisms and communication strategies.
The results of this study provide significant insights into the preferences and trade-offs that EV drivers in China consider when 
evaluating V2G technology. Policymakers and businesses can enhance consumer acceptance and satisfaction by balancing trade-offs 
and designing appealing V2G contracts based on results of this study. For example, appropriate economic incentives could be designed 
to accelerate the transition to widespread V2G implementation. Increasing annual revenue from approximately ¥1000 to ¥5500 can 
offset the 9.1% decline in participation and speed mass uptake. Beyond direct subsidy, institutional and market reforms such as refined 
time-of-use or real-time pricing schemes should be integrated into China’s ongoing electricity-market reform to make short-term 
subsidies evolve into long-term and sustainable market participation. While financial inducements remain a prerequisite, opera­
tional conditions ultimately shape uptake. Reducing the range guarantee to 100 km lowers participation probability by 5.7%. Setting a 
higher guaranteed minimum range will alleviate range anxiety, encouraging more users to participate without compromising their 
mobility. Besides, participants require compensation of nearly ¥6,856 per year for 16 h daily plug-in time, indicating a strong aversion 
to extensive charging obligations. Reducing the plug-in requirement to 4 h increases participation by 15.7%. Contracts that minimize 
interference with daily schedules will therefore be more attractive and cost-effective. Additionally, standardized and transparent 
battery health management systems, including clear degradation warranties and insurance coverage, can substantially enhance user 
confidence. Results show that drivers are willing to pay ¥2,257 per year to extend warranty coverage from 6 to 9 years. Increasing the 
battery-capacity guarantee from 70% to 85% yields a WTA of ¥2,224 per year. Policymakers could implement reasonable V2G battery 
insurance to protect users’ assets and reduce participation risk. Once these technical and regulatory foundations are in place, policy 
efforts can progressively shift toward psychological and informational engagement. The latent variable “Benefits” is significant.

Comprehensive information campaigns can bridge knowledge gaps, thus cultivating a supportive consumer base. Collaboration be­
tween government agencies, utilities, and EV manufacturers will be essential for creating coherent, trustworthy narratives around 
significance of V2G. Furthermore, the latent variable “Concerns” captures worries about operation. Data governance should be 
strengthened. Since V2G operation involves continuous information exchange between vehicles, chargers, and grid operators, clear 
rules on data privacy, ownership, and cybersecurity are needed to reduce user concerns and facilitate cross-sector collaboration. Given 
that “Technology affinity” significantly enhances the likelihood of V2G participation, targeted communication strategies are essential 
to improve the efficiency of information dissemination and accelerate early-stage market uptake. In particular, advertising and 
outreach efforts should be prioritised toward individuals who already exhibit high interest in digital technologies or smart-energy

In conclusion, this study has laid the groundwork for a deeper understanding of the public's willingness to participate in V2G. The 
insights gained from this study carry profound implications for policymakers and practitioners in the field of V2G technology. By 
understanding the public's preferences for V2G contracts, more attractive and competitive contracts and services can be developed, 
fostering the development and upgrading of the V2G industry chain and enhancing China's global competitiveness in this field.
