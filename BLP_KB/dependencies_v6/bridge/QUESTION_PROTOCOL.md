# Question-Led Top-Journal Hypothesis Packaging Protocol

## Purpose

Use this protocol to convert top-journal paper memories and anchored source packs into reusable economics-theory and hypothesis-design memory. This is not a generic paper summary exercise.

## Evidence boundary

- Start from the individual AER third-pass paper memory.
- Reopen the matching source pack for every paper.
- Inspect the original artifact or PDF when a theoretical primitive, equation, proposition, quotation, table, or exact layout claim is consequential and the source pack is insufficient.
- Label the card as `source-verified`, `source-pack anchored`, `card-backed`, or `inference`.
- A completed bridge card does not by itself upgrade the underlying paper to fully read. Preserve the corpus completion status recorded by the source package.

## Questions every paper must answer

1. What visible object, puzzle, policy debate, or standard empirical object opens the paper?
2. What canonical economics theory, benchmark model, or accepted result gives the reader a baseline?
3. Which primitive, constraint, information set, market boundary, equilibrium margin, or behavioral assumption is held fixed in that benchmark?
4. What omitted wedge or changed primitive makes the benchmark incomplete?
5. Who are the agents; what are their actions, information, constraints, payoffs, and timing?
6. Write the full mechanism chain as:
   `shock/rule -> information/constraint/incentive -> action -> market/institutional response -> outcome`.
7. What proposition, directional hypothesis, sign restriction, heterogeneity prediction, or equilibrium implication follows? State the conditions.
8. Which variables or contrasts directly measure each theoretical object, and which are only proxies?
9. What is the closest rival mechanism? What result would distinguish, weaken, or falsify the preferred chain?
10. What part is theory, what part is mechanism-consistent evidence, and what part is causally or structurally identified?
11. Why is prose enough, why is a formal model needed, or why is a structural model needed?
12. How is the hypothesis packaged rhetorically?
    - opening benchmark;
    - tension or wedge sentence;
    - bridge from mechanism to prediction;
    - hypothesis/proposition sentence;
    - contribution sentence;
    - boundary or caveat.
13. What is the reusable packaging move, and when would copying it be misleading?
14. Which specialist agents should reuse this paper, and for what decision?

## Card schema

Each card must contain:

- metadata and evidence status;
- source paths and anchors;
- visible object and canonical benchmark;
- fixed primitive and omitted wedge;
- primitives and timing;
- auditable mechanism chain;
- proposition/hypothesis block;
- observable and estimand mapping;
- rival mechanisms and falsification;
- formal/structural-model decision;
- hypothesis-packaging sequence;
- reusable move and misuse warning;
- agent-routing tags.

## Agent routing

- `economic_foundations_professor`: canonical theory and primitive crosswalk.
- `theory_professor_1`: baseline model, mechanism, comparative statics, predictions.
- `theory_professor_2`: rival mechanisms, fragile assumptions, boundaries, falsification.
- `hypothesis_packager`: mechanism-to-hypothesis and rhetorical packaging.
- `formal_model_designer`: minimal formal model and proposition discipline.
- `structural_professor`: equilibrium, welfare, dynamics, and counterfactual necessity.
- `blp_professor`: differentiated-product demand/supply and BLP-specific extensions.
- `literature_scout`: nearest benchmark, gap, evidence status, source follow-up.
- `identification_referee`: observable, estimand, identification, and claim boundary.
- `policy_background_professor`: implementation, gatekeepers, treatment versions.
- `idea_generator`: corrected object, wedge, contribution, data feasibility.
- `writing_integrator`: paper-ready theory and hypothesis prose.
- `economics_theory_agent`: conflict resolution and final synthesis.
